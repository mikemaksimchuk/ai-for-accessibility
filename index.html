Six things to settle before any of this reaches a student

These are the failure points that turn an accessibility gain into a compliance problem.

AI output is a draft, not an accommodation of record.

If an IEP specifies a qualified interpreter, live captions do not satisfy it. If it specifies braille instruction, a screen reader does not satisfy it. AI reduces the labor of producing accessible material. It does not change what the plan legally requires.

Auto-generated alt text and captions need a human pass.

Generated alt text describes what is in the picture. It does not know why you assigned the picture. The instructional point is the part a person has to add.

Student data needs an agreement before it goes anywhere.

IEPs, 504 plans, evaluation reports, behavior data, and health information are education records. Confirm a signed data privacy agreement covers the specific tool before any of it is pasted into a prompt. Every prompt here uses bracketed placeholders for that reason.

Never let AI issue a standalone label.

Risk flags, behavior classifications, disability predictions, and language proficiency calls stay with the team. AI can organize the evidence a team reviews. It does not get a vote.

WCAG conformance is the floor, not the goal.

A page can pass every automated check and still be unusable. Test with the people who rely on it, and budget for that testing the same way you budget for the license.

Translation quality varies sharply by language.

Machine translation performs well in high-resource languages and poorly in many of the languages Michigan families actually speak. Verify before relying on it for anything consequential.

Current federal deadline, as of September 2026

The ADA Title II digital accessibility rule still requires WCAG 2.1 Level AA for web content and mobile apps a public entity provides or makes available, including through vendors. On April 20, 2026, the Department of Justice issued an interim final rule extending the compliance dates by one year: April 26, 2027 for public entities serving a total population of 50,000 or more, and April 26, 2028 for smaller entities and special district governments.

The standard, the scope, and the exceptions did not change — only the dates. Existing Title II effective-communication obligations apply now, and private litigation is unaffected by the extension. Districts should confirm with counsel which date applies to them, since the threshold turns on the population of the jurisdiction served.
